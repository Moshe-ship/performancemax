But when Mike looked at his calendar on a Tuesday morning, he saw three empty bays. When he looked at his bank account, he saw a $2,500 monthly agency fee leaving his account, but he couldn't actually point to which jobs were paying for it. 

He felt like he was gambling. He knew the phone was ringing more than it did five years ago, but he didn't know if it was ringing because of the agency or because of word-of-mouth. He was paying for "traffic," but traffic doesn't pay the rent. Booked jobs pay the rent.

Here is the only way to tell if your marketing is actually working. We call it the **Bank Account Test.**

### The Lie of the "Vanity Metric"

Before we get into the test, we need to clear the air on the jargon agencies use to hide the fact that they aren't making you money.

If your agency tells you "traffic is up 40%," that means absolutely nothing. You can have 10,000 people visit your website, but if none of them are looking for a full-body PPF wrap or a plumbing overhaul, you have 10,000 people wasting your bandwidth.

If they talk about "impressions," they are telling you how many people *saw* your ad. They aren't telling you how many people *called* you.

The only metrics that matter for a local service business are:
1. **Qualified Calls:** The phone rings, and the person on the other end actually needs the service you provide in the area you serve.
2. **Booked Jobs:** The call turns into a date on the calendar.
3. **Closed Revenue:** The job gets done, and the check clears.

Everything else is just noise.

### The Bank Account Test: How to Actually Track Your Money

To pass the Bank Account Test, you have to stop guessing. You can't just "feel" like the marketing is working. You need a system that connects the dollar you spend on an ad directly to the dollar that hits your bank account.

Here is how you do it without needing a degree in data science.

#### 1. The "How Did You Hear About Us?" Audit
This is the simplest tool in the shed, but most owners ignore it. Every single person who calls your shop needs to be asked: *"Just curious, how did you find us?"*

Don't just nod and move on. Write it down. Use a simple spreadsheet or a notebook. 
- "Google Search"
- "Saw your ad on Facebook"
- "Referral from [Name]"
- "Saw the truck in the neighborhood"

If you are spending $2,000 a month on Google Ads, but 90% of your new jobs are coming from referrals, your marketing isn't working. You're paying for a ghost.

#### 2. Dedicated Phone Lines (The "Truth" Line)
If you want to know exactly what your marketing is doing, stop using your main office line for everything. 

Use a dedicated tracking number for your ads. When a lead clicks an ad and calls a specific number that forwards to your phone, you know *exactly* where that call came from. 

When that call turns into a $2,000 ceramic coating job, you can look at your ad spend and say, "I spent $50 to get this call, and it made me $2,000." Now you aren't guessing. You're calculating.

#### 3. The Calendar Match
At the end of every month, look at your booked jobs. Then, look at your marketing spend.

If you spent $1,500 on marketing and you booked five new jobs with an average ticket of $800, you brought in $4,000 in gross revenue. Your "Cost Per Acquisition" was $300 per job. 

Is that a win? For a window tint shop, maybe. For a full-scale HVAC installation, it's a home run. For a basic oil change, you're losing money. 

The Bank Account Test is simple: **Is the revenue generated by these specific leads significantly higher than the cost to get them?** If the answer is "I don't know," you are overpaying for your marketing.

### How Much Should You Actually Be Spending?

One of the biggest points of friction between owners and agencies is the budget. Agencies want you to spend more so they can make more. Owners want to spend as little as possible.

The truth is in the math of your business. To stay competitive and keep your crew fully booked without burning yourself out, there are real benchmarks you can use.

**For shops doing under $1M in annual revenue:**
Typically, you should allocate **5% to 10% of your gross revenue** toward marketing. If you're doing $500k a year, spending $2,500 to $4,000 a month to keep the pipeline full is a healthy range.

**For shops doing between $1M and $3M in annual revenue:**
At this stage, you're likely looking to scale or dominate your local market. Your spend typically moves up to **8% to 12% of revenue**. 

Why the increase? Because as you grow, you aren't just fighting for "anyone who needs a job"—you're fighting for the *best* jobs. You're paying to filter out the price-shoppers and attract the high-ticket clients who don't blink at a premium quote.

If you are spending 20% of your revenue on marketing, you're likely being ripped off or your conversion process is broken. If you're spending 1%, you're relying entirely on luck and referrals, which means you don't actually own your growth—you're just renting it.

### The Red Flag: When the Agency Deflects

When you start asking these questions, a bad agency will get defensive. They will try to move the goalposts.

They'll say: *"Well, we're building your 'top-of-funnel awareness.' It takes time for the brand to resonate."*

**Translation:** "We are spending your money, but we can't prove it's working."

They'll say: *"The CTR (Click-Through Rate) is the highest it's ever been!"*

**Translation:** "People are clicking the ad, but they hate your offer or your website is a mess, and they aren't calling you."

A real partner—one who respects the trade—will welcome the Bank Account Test. They will say, *"I don't care about the clicks either. Let's look at the call logs and the booked jobs. If the phone isn't ringing, we change the ad. If the phone is ringing but they aren't booking, we change the offer."*

### Stop Renting Your Leads, Start Owning Your Phone

The hardest lesson for most local business owners is that **buying leads is a losing game.** 

When you pay a lead service, you are renting a customer. You don't own the relationship, you don't own the data, and as the HomeAdvisor scandal proved, you can't even trust that the lead is exclusive to you.

The goal is to build a system where *you* own the phone line. When a customer searches for "best PPF shop in Lewisville" or "emergency plumber in Cary," they should find *your* business, click *your* link, and call *your* phone.

When you own the lead generation process, you can turn the faucet up when you have an empty calendar and turn it down when your crew is slammed. That is the only way to actually scale a service business without losing your mind.
