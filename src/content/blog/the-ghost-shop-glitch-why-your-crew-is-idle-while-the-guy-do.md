### The "Lead Buyer" Lie

Before we get into the fix, we need to address the elephant in the room. A lot of you have been burned by the big lead-gen sites. You’ve probably seen the news about the FTC hitting HomeAdvisor and Angi for millions of dollars because they were selling the same lead to five different contractors, or worse, selling you leads for jobs that weren't even in your service area.

It’s a scam. You pay for a "lead," and by the time you call them, the customer has already hired someone else, or they’re looking for a price that wouldn't even cover your gas to get to the job site.

The goal isn't to "buy leads." The goal is to **own your leads**. When a customer searches for your trade in your town, they should find *you* directly. When they call your shop's number, that's a lead you own. You didn't pay a middleman a $50 fee for the privilege of fighting over a scrap.

### Why You’re Invisible (And How to Fix It This Week)

If your shop isn't showing up, it's usually not because of some mysterious "algorithm update." It's usually because of one of three concrete friction points. Here is how to knock them out this week.

#### 1. The "Wrong Address" Trap
Google doesn't care how good your PPF is; it cares where you are. If your business address on your website doesn't match your Google Business Profile (GBP) exactly—down to the "St." vs "Street"—Google gets confused. If Google is confused, it won't risk putting you in the "Map Pack" (those top three businesses that show up with the map).

**The Fix:** 
Spend 10 minutes today. Open your website, open your Google Business Profile, and open your Facebook page. Make sure the Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP) are identical on all three. If one says "Suite 100" and the other says "#100," fix it. This isn't about "branding"; it's about making sure Google knows exactly which shop to send the phone call to.

#### 2. The "Silent Shop" Syndrome
Google prioritizes businesses that look "alive." If the last photo you uploaded to your profile was from 2022, Google assumes you might be out of business or just don't care. 

A customer looking for a contractor in Cary, NC, doesn't want to see a stock photo of a wrench. They want to see a photo of a job you finished yesterday. They want to see your crew in the field. They want to see the finished tint job on a 2024 Porsche.

**The Fix:** 
Every single time your crew finishes a job this week, take three photos. One of the process, one of the finished result, and one of the happy customer (if they're game). Upload those directly to your Google Business Profile. Do this three times a week. It tells Google your shop is active, which pushes you higher in the rankings, which puts more jobs on the calendar.

#### 3. The "Review Gap"
We know you're too busy working to beg for reviews. But here is the reality: the guy with 4.8 stars and 150 reviews will get the phone call over the guy with 5 stars and only 3 reviews every single time. 

The problem is that most owners wait until the end of the month to ask for reviews, or they forget entirely. By then, the customer has moved on.

**The Fix:** 
Create a "Review Shortcut." Use a free tool to create a direct link to your Google review page. Save that link in your phone's notes or as a shortcut. The second you finish the job and the customer says, "Looks great!", you say: *"Glad you love it. Do me a huge favor—I'm trying to grow the shop locally. Can I text you a link to leave a quick review?"* 

Send the link before you leave the driveway. That is how you bridge the gap and start stealing the leads from the competitor down the street.

### The Math of Marketing: Stop Guessing Your Budget

One of the biggest reasons shop owners get burned by agencies is that they don't have a benchmark for what they should be spending. They just throw $1,000 or $2,000 a month at a "manager" and hope the phone rings.

Marketing is an investment in your crew's payroll. If you aren't tracking it against your revenue, you're just gambling. 

Here is the straight-talk benchmark for local service businesses:

*   **The Sub-$1M Shop:** If you're doing under $1M in annual revenue, you should typically be spending **5-10% of your gross revenue** on marketing. If you're doing $500k, that's $25k–$50k a year. If you're spending way less, you're wondering why the calendar is empty. If you're spending way more, you're likely wasting money on "vanity metrics."
*   **The $1M–$3M Shop:** Once you've scaled, your efficiency usually improves, but your competition increases. You should be budgeting **8-12% of revenue**. At this stage, you aren't just looking for "more calls"—you're looking for *better* jobs. You want the high-ticket PPF packages, not the cheap window tints that take up a bay for four hours.

If your agency is showing you a chart that says "Traffic is up 40%," but your booked jobs haven't moved, you aren't spending 10% of your revenue on marketing—you're donating it to the agency.

### Accountability Over "Impressions"

We've all heard the jargon. "CTR," "Impressions," "Keyword Density." None of that pays your installers. None of that buys a new piece of equipment for the shop.

The only metrics that matter to a business owner are:
1.  **How many times did the phone ring?**
2.  **How many of those calls turned into a booked job?**
3.  **What was the total invoice value of those jobs?**

If a marketing effort doesn't lead to those three things, it's a failure. Period.

When we work with shops, we don't care about "brand awareness." We care about filling the bays. If we run an ad campaign for a carpenter in Raleigh and it gets 10,000 views but zero phone calls, we didn't win. We failed.

### Your Checklist for This Friday

You don't need a $5,000-a-month retainer to start seeing a difference. If you want to stop being the "Ghost Shop," do these four things before the weekend hits:

1.  **Audit your NAP:** Make sure your name, address, and phone number are identical on your website, Google, and Facebook.
2.  **The "Freshness" Push:** Upload 5 real photos of your work from the last 7 days to your Google Business Profile.
3.  **The Review Blitz:** Reach out to your last five happy customers and send them that direct review link.
4.  **The Math Check:** Look at your total revenue for the last quarter. Calculate 5-10% of that. That is your marketing budget. If you're spending more than that without a massive increase in booked jobs, fire your agency.
