### Why Your Shop Isn't Showing Up (The Real Reasons)

If your crew is sitting idle while the guy down the street is slammed, it usually comes down to three specific failures. None of these have to do with "SEO magic"—they have to do with how Google decides who to trust.

#### 1. The "NAP" Nightmare
NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number. It sounds simple, but if your shop is listed as "Mike’s Tint Shop" on Facebook, "Mike’s Auto Glass & PPF" on Yelp, and "Mike’s Tinting" on your website, Google gets confused. 

Google isn't a human; it's a machine. If the data doesn't match exactly, Google doesn't want to risk sending a paying customer to a business that might not exist or has moved. If your info is inconsistent, Google pushes you down the list and puts the guy who has his act together at the top.

#### 2. The Review Gap
We aren't talking about having a "few" 5-star reviews from your cousins. We're talking about *velocity* and *recency*. 

If your last review was from six months ago, Google thinks you might be out of business. Even if you have 100 reviews, if the shop down the street has 20 reviews but five of them came in the last two weeks, Google is going to prioritize them. They want to show the user a business that is active *right now*.

#### 3. The "Ghost" Profile
A lot of owners set up their Google Business Profile (GBP) once and then never touch it again. They treat it like a digital business card. 

Google treats your profile like a social media feed. If you aren't posting photos of the jobs you finished yesterday, if you aren't updating your hours, and if you aren't answering the questions people are asking in the "Q&A" section, you are telling Google your business is stagnant.

### How to Fix It This Week (The No-BS Blueprint)

You don't need a six-month "strategy phase." You need the phone to ring. Here is exactly what you can do by Friday to start moving the needle.

#### Step 1: The Consistency Audit (Do this Monday)
Spend one hour searching for your business across the web. Check your website, Facebook, Yelp, YellowPages, and any local directories. 
*   Is the phone number the same? 
*   Is the address spelled exactly the same way? 
*   Is the business name identical?

If it's not, fix it. Every single one. This tells Google, "Yes, this is a real, stable business located at this exact spot."

#### Step 2: The "Fresh Work" Dump (Do this Tuesday)
Stop taking "pretty" photos for Instagram and start taking "proof" photos for Google. 
*   Take a photo of the finished PPF job on that Porsche.
*   Take a photo of the new HVAC unit you just installed in that living room.
*   Take a photo of your crew in their uniforms in front of the shop.

Upload 5-10 of these photos directly to your Google Business Profile. Caption them with the specific service and the city. Example: *"Full front-end PPF install for a client in Lewisville, TX."* This tells Google you are active and you serve that specific area.

#### Step 3: The Review Push (Do this Wednesday & Thursday)
You have happy customers. The problem is they forget to tell Google. 
Stop asking them "if" they can leave a review. Start making it part of the job. When you hand over the keys or finish the walkthrough, say: *"I'm glad you're happy with the work. It helps my crew get more jobs when people leave a review. Can I text you a link right now?"*

Send that link via text before they leave your driveway. One review on a Thursday can literally trigger a new lead on a Friday.

#### Step 4: The "Direct Line" Check (Do this Friday)
Look at your website on your phone. If a customer has to click three times or fill out a long, annoying contact form to get a quote, they are going to bounce and call the next guy.

Put your phone number in a big, bold button at the very top of the page. Make it "click-to-call." In the home services world, the business that answers the phone the fastest usually wins the job. Don't make them work to find you.

### The Math: How Much Should You Actually Spend?

One of the biggest frustrations we hear from owners is that they don't know if they're being ripped off by their marketing agency. They see a bill for $2,000 a month and have no idea if it's working.

Here is the honest benchmark for local service businesses. Marketing is an investment in growth, not a cost of doing business.

*   **For shops doing under $1M in annual revenue:** You should typically be spending **5% to 10%** of your gross revenue on marketing. If you're doing $500k, that's roughly $2,500 to $4,000 a month.
*   **For shops doing $1M to $3M:** Your spend usually bumps up to **8% to 12%**. At this stage, you aren't just looking for "more calls"; you're looking to dominate your market and push out the competition.

If you are spending 20% of your revenue on marketing and your calendar is still empty, you don't have a budget problem—you have a strategy problem. You're likely paying for "vanity metrics" (like impressions) instead of "booked jobs."
